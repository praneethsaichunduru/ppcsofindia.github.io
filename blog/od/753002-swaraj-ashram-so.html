Swaraj Ashram

ସ୍ୱରାଜ ଆଶ୍ରମ

The Swaraj Ashram of Cuttack was set up in March 1921, in the early months of the Non-Cooperation Movement. It was founded by Odisha Congress leaders as a place to train volunteers and workers for the movement, and it became a base for the Congress in the region. The ashram was one of several of its kind, and a similar one, the Alaka Ashram, was established at Jagatsinghpur at about the same time.

Its best known moment came in March 1921, when Mahatma Gandhi paid his first visit to Odisha. He came on the invitation of Gopabandhu Das, the poet and social reformer known as Utkalmani, and he stayed at the Swaraj Ashram during his visit to Cuttack. A large procession took him from the railway station to the ashram. He addressed big gatherings, including one on the sandy bed of the Kathajodi river, and visited the Satyabadi Bana Vidyalaya, the school founded by Gopabandhu Das, before he left for Puri.

The visit gave a strong push to the Non-Cooperation Movement in Odisha. Gandhi came to the state several more times in the following decades, and Cuttack remained an important centre of the freedom movement. Other leaders who gathered around the ashram included Gopabandhu Das, who had devoted his life to the service of the people of Odisha, and the volunteers who worked with him in the villages.

The ashram stands as a reminder of how the national movement reached the towns of Odisha in the early twentieth century. Its name, Swaraj, meaning self-rule, was the aim of the movement, and the cancellation brings that period into the postal history of Cuttack.

The pictorial cancellation is held at the Swaraj Ashram Sub Post Office, which takes its name from the ashram.
