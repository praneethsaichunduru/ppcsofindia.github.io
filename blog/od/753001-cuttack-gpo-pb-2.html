Barabati Fort

ବାରବାଟି ଦୁର୍ଗ

Barabati Fort is the historic fort of Cuttack, the city that served as the capital of Odisha for centuries and remained the capital of the state until 1948. Its origins are debated. Some accounts trace a first fortification to the Somavamshi ruler Marakata Keshari, who built a stone embankment to protect the city in about 989 CE. More commonly, the fort is credited to the Ganga king Anangabhima Deva III, who made Cuttack his capital in the early thirteenth century under the name Varanasi Kataka, though the exact date is argued over by historians.

Under the Gajapati kings, who ruled Odisha from the fifteenth century, Cuttack remained the capital, and the fort was the seat of their power. Later it passed to the Afghan and Mughal governors, then to the Marathas, who ruled the region from the middle of the eighteenth century, and finally to the British in 1803. Each of these powers left its mark on the fort, which stood at the heart of the history of the region for many centuries.

Today little of the original structure survives. The fort was surrounded by a wide moat, and its massive stone gateway, a part of the outer walls and the moat remain, and are protected by the Archaeological Survey of India. Local accounts speak of a great palace of many storeys inside the walls, which has not survived. The area inside the fort has since been given to other uses, and the Barabati Stadium, one of the best known cricket grounds in the country, stands on part of the ground.

This is the second pictorial cancellation of the Cuttack General Post Office to show the fort. The first, introduced in 2007, paired the fort with a figure of Odissi dance, while the cancellation of 2026 gives it a place of its own.
