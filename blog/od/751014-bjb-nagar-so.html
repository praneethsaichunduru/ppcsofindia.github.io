Rajarani Temple

ରାଜାରାଣୀ ମନ୍ଦିର

The Rajarani Temple is one of the best known temples of Bhubaneswar, and it is unusual in two ways. It is a Shaiva temple, as the figures of Shiva and Parvati on its platform and the guardians at its doorway show, but no image has been installed in its sanctum, and no worship is held there. It is dated by most scholars to the eleventh century, and early writers placed the start of its construction at about 1100 CE.

Its name is also unusual, since Rajarani does not refer to a king and queen. It comes from the local name of the dull red and golden yellow sandstone from which the temple is built, which gives its walls a warm colour that differs from the darker stone of the older temples of the city. The temple was once known as Indreswara, and historians debate whether the name came from a person, Indraratha, or from a title of Shiva.

Architecturally, it is a fine example of the Kalinga style, with a curved tower rising over the sanctum and a hall in front of it. The tower is ringed by small copies of itself, as though it were surrounded by smaller peaks, and the walls carry carved guardians of the directions, graceful female figures and flowing scrolls. The sculpture is often seen as an anticipation of the great temples that followed, especially the Lingaraja and the temples at Konark and Puri.

The temple is now maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India and stands in a landscaped garden. It is the setting for an annual music festival, usually held in January, which brings classical musicians to perform in front of the temple.

The pictorial cancellation at B J B Nagar Sub Post Office shows this temple and adds one of the best loved monuments of Bhubaneswar to the list of PPCs of the state.
