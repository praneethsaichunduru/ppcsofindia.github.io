Nandankanan Zoological Park

ନନ୍ଦନକାନନ ଜୀବଜନ୍ତୁ ଉଦ୍ୟାନ

Nandankanan, whose name means the garden of heaven, is a zoo and botanical garden set in a forest near Barang, about 15 to 20 kilometres from Bhubaneswar. It opened on 29 December 1960 as a biological park and was given the name Nandankanan Zoological Park in 1981. It covers a large area of natural sal forest on the edge of the Kanjia lake, and the park's setting among trees gives its enclosures a more natural look than many city zoos.

The park is best known for its white tigers. The first white tiger of the park was born in 1980, and the white tiger safari, opened in 1991, allows visitors to see the animals in a large forest enclosure from a vehicle. Nandankanan has since become a leading centre for the breeding of white tigers, and the park is also known for its work on other threatened animals, including the gharial and several kinds of birds.

Nandankanan has a number of firsts to its credit. In 2009 it became the first zoo in India to become a member of the World Association of Zoos and Aquariums. It combines a zoo with a botanical garden and a lake, and visitors can cross Kanjia lake by boat or cross it on a ropeway. The park draws large crowds throughout the year, with school groups and families among its most frequent visitors.

The park is run by the Odisha forest department, and it is open on most days of the week and closed on one weekday, so visitors should check the timings and the season before they go.

The pictorial cancellation at Barang Sub Post Office carries the best known zoo of Odisha to the collectors of the country, and it adds a wildlife subject to the long list of temples, forts and monuments that make up the PPCs of the state.
