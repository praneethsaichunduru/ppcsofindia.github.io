Shanti Stupa

ଶାନ୍ତି ସ୍ତୂପ

The Shanti Stupa, or Peace Pagoda, stands on the Dhauli hills on the bank of the Daya river, about eight kilometres from Bhubaneswar. The hills are traditionally associated with the Kalinga War of about 261 BCE, in which the Mauryan emperor Ashoka defeated the kingdom of Kalinga. The war left a very heavy toll of lives, and Ashoka's own edicts record his remorse and his turn towards the path of dhamma, which made the site one of the best known places in the story of Buddhism in India.

The stupa itself is modern. It was built in 1972 by the Japan Buddha Sangha and the Kalinga Nippon Buddha Sangha, and the driving force behind it was the Japanese monk Nichidatsu Fujii, known as Fuji Guruji, who raised peace pagodas in several countries. The white dome is topped with chattras, or umbrellas, and the base carries panels showing scenes from the life of the Buddha. From the top of the hill, the dome is seen against the green plain of Khordha district.

Close to the stupa, on a rock beside the path, are the Dhauli edicts of Ashoka, including a set of messages meant for the people of the newly conquered region, in which he describes the people of Kalinga as his children. Above the edicts is a rock-cut elephant, among the oldest pieces of sculpture in Odisha, which is thought to date from the time of Ashoka and which rises from the stone as if emerging from the rock.

The hills are quiet, and the stupa has become a place where visitors come for the view as well as for the history. It is especially popular in the evening, when the white dome catches the light, and on days of Buddhist observance, when local groups and monks gather there.

The pictorial cancellation is held at Gopinathpur Branch Post Office, close to the Dhauli area, and it joins the list of Odisha PPCs that mark sites from the history of Buddhism in Kalinga.
