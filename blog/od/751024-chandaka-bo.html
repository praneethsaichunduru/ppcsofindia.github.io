Chandaka Elephant Reserve

ଚନ୍ଦକା ହାତୀ ସଂରକ୍ଷିତ ଅଞ୍ଚଳ

The Chandaka Elephant Reserve is a forest area on the north-western edge of Bhubaneswar, lying across the districts of Khordha and Cuttack. It was declared an elephant reserve in 1982, and the Chandaka-Dampara Wildlife Sanctuary was notified in 1985. The official figure for the notified area is about 193 square kilometres, and the forest is one of the few places where a large wild animal lives within reach of a capital city.

The vegetation is dry deciduous forest with patches of scrub, grassland and sal, on low hills cut by streams. The Deras reservoir inside the sanctuary and the Dampara area attract birds and animals, and the forest is home to elephants, leopards, spotted deer, wild boar, and many kinds of reptiles and birds. The elephants are the best known residents, and their movement through the forest, and between it and neighbouring areas, is a subject of constant attention.

Chandaka's closeness to the city is both its charm and its problem. Roads, quarries, new settlements and fields have cut into the paths that elephants once used to travel to other forests, and the animals sometimes enter villages in search of food, which has led to conflict with local people. Forest officials and conservation groups have worked to protect the remaining corridors, to compensate farmers for crop losses, and to educate people about living near elephants.

Visitors can go to the sanctuary through the forest department, which controls entry and offers a limited set of guided visits. The forest is a popular place for nature lovers from Bhubaneswar, and it is a reminder that Odisha has one of the largest elephant populations of any state.

The pictorial cancellation at Chandaka Branch Post Office carries the elephant of the reserve into the postal system of the capital region, and it celebrates a forest that has survived at the edge of a growing city.
