Western Tragopan

पश्चिमी ट्रैगोपैन (जुजुराना)

The western tragopan (Tragopan melanocephalus), known in parts of Himachal Pradesh as the jujurana, is one of the most striking pheasants of the western Himalaya. The name comes from Greek words for a goat and the god Pan, a reference to the two fleshy blue horns that the male raises during courtship. The male is easily recognised by its black crown, red neck and breast, and a body covered in rows of white-edged spots, while the female is a mottled brown that blends into the forest floor. In 2007 Himachal Pradesh adopted the bird as its state bird.

The species lives between roughly 2,400 and 3,600 metres, in dense temperate and sub-alpine forests of oak, rhododendron and conifers with a thick undergrowth of ringal bamboo. In India it is found in the Kashmir region and in the higher forests of districts such as Kullu and Shimla. The upper forest zone of the Great Himalayan National Park is regarded as holding the largest known population. The bird is shy and difficult to spot, and it is far more often heard, through its loud, wailing call in spring, than seen.

The western tragopan is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN. Its main threats are loss and fragmentation of forest, hunting, grazing and disturbance in its breeding grounds, and a changing climate that may push suitable habitat higher up the slopes. A conservation breeding programme at the pheasantry in Sarahan, in Shimla district, has worked on breeding the bird in captivity so that it can support wild numbers, and the bird has become a flagship for the protection of Himalayan forests.

Summer Hill is one of the hills that make up Shimla, set at an altitude of a little over 2,100 metres a few kilometres west of the Ridge. It is a quiet, wooded residential neighbourhood known for its old colonial-era houses and for the campus of Himachal Pradesh University, and it has a station on the narrow-gauge Kalka-Shimla Railway, which is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Mountain Railways of India. The hill is also associated with Mahatma Gandhi, who stayed in the Summer Hill area during visits to Shimla, the first of them in 1921.

The pictorial cancellation at Summerhill Sub Post Office carries the state bird of Himachal Pradesh to a hill-station neighbourhood. The tragopan itself lives in the high forests of the district rather than in the town, but the cancellation links the two, and gives collectors who visit Shimla a reason to stop at one of its quieter post offices.
