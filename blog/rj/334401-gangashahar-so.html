Acharya Tulsi Shakti Peeth

आचार्य तुलसी शक्ति पीठ

Acharya Tulsi Shakti Peeth takes its name from Acharya Tulsi, born Tulsiram Khater in 1914 at Ladnun in Rajasthan's Nagaur district, who was initiated into Jain monkhood in 1926 under the Shwetambar Terapanth tradition. In 1936, at Gangapur's Rang Bhawan, his predecessor Acharya Kalugani named him successor, making him, at just twenty-two, the ninth Acharya of the Terapanth order, then comprising some 139 monks and 333 nuns. The 'Shakti Peeth' designation, meaning a seat of spiritual power or energy, was adopted by the Terapanth community to mark Gangashahar as the place inseparably linked with the final chapter of his life and teaching.

The site's significance rests chiefly on its connection to Acharya Tulsi's death: in 1997 he announced he would observe his chaturmas, the traditional four-month monsoon retreat of Jain ascetics, at Gangashahar, and he passed away there on 23 June 1997 at the age of eighty-two. Gangashahar itself held earlier importance for the Terapanth movement, having been home to some of the earliest followers who took the Anuvrat vows after Tulsi launched the movement in 1949. The Peeth is thus regarded less as an ancient pilgrimage shrine and more as a memorial and living institution tied to a reformist leader whose influence shaped twentieth-century Jain social and religious life across India.

Acharya Tulsi's wider legacy, which the Shakti Peeth is built to commemorate and extend, rests on the Anuvrat ('small vow') movement he inaugurated on 2 March 1949, calling on people of all faiths to adopt limited, lay versions of the Jain monastic vows of truth, non-violence, non-possession and self-restraint as a framework for ethical living in ordinary society. During his roughly fifty-eight years as Acharya, he also guided the founding of Jain Vishva Bharati at Ladnun in 1971, which the University Grants Commission recognised as a deemed university in 1991, and he authored or inspired over a hundred texts on Jain philosophy, conduct and social reform.

A distinguishing feature of his tenure, continued and commemorated through institutions linked to the Shakti Peeth, was the creation of the Samani order in 1980, a unique category of semi-ascetic Jain women permitted to travel by modern transport and interact more freely with lay society than fully initiated nuns, enabling them to teach Jain philosophy, yoga and meditation abroad, including at Jain Vishva Bharati's international study centres. Acharya Tulsi was also honoured nationally during his lifetime, receiving the title 'Anuvrat Anushasta' and being felicitated by dignitaries including then Vice-President Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan in 1962 at Gangashahar itself on the occasion of his Acharyaship's silver jubilee, underscoring the town's long-standing ties to his public recognition.

In the years since his death, the Terapanth Sangha, now led by his successors in an unbroken line of Acharyas, has maintained the Shakti Peeth at Gangashahar as a functioning centre for religious instruction, meditation and community gatherings rather than a static memorial, drawing Terapanthi pilgrims and Anuvrat followers particularly around the anniversary of his death each June. The Government of India also marked his birth centenary in 2014 with a commemorative coin and postage stamp, reflecting his recognised standing beyond the Jain community alone. The Shakti Peeth, together with the Gangashahar post office that now carries a pictorial cancellation in his name, keeps the locality identified first and foremost with the Acharya who spent his last months and died there.
