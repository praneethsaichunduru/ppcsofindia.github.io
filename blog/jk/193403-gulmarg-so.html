Gondola Ropeway

گونڈولا رَسہٕ گاڑی

The Gulmarg Gondola did not originate from any ancient legend but from a modern state initiative to open the high meadows above Gulmarg, long prized by the British as a hill retreat and golfing station, to year-round tourism. Conceived as a joint venture between the Jammu and Kashmir government and the French engineering firm Pomagalski, the project was intended to carry visitors from the valley floor at Gulmarg directly onto the shoulder of Apharwat Peak, a feat that had previously demanded a strenuous trek or pony ride. Planning stretched back to the late 1980s, but it was only in the 1990s that construction proper began, giving the ropeway its present name, the Gulmarg Gondola, after the aerial tramway technology it employs.

As a piece of transport and tourism infrastructure, the gondola holds a distinctive place among the world's high-altitude cable car systems, with its upper terminus near 3,980 metres making it one of the highest-reaching passenger ropeways anywhere and, by some rankings, the highest ski lift in operation globally. Its significance is both economic and recreational: the lift underpins Gulmarg's transformation from a seasonal summer resort into a major winter sports hub, feeding a ski and snowboard area that spans roughly 25 kilometres of marked slopes and several other connecting lifts. It is owned and operated by the Jammu & Kashmir Cable Car Corporation, a state-run body, making the gondola as much an instrument of regional economic policy as a tourist attraction.

The ropeway was built in two distinct phases. Phase one, completed in 1998, linked the Gulmarg base station to Kongdoori at roughly 2,650 metres. Phase two, a 2.5-kilometre stretch engineered to climb to the Apharwat shoulder at about 3,950 to 3,980 metres, was completed by Pomagalski using 36 cabins strung across 18 towers and was formally inaugurated on 28 May 2005 after construction delays tied to the terrain and the region's security situation. The cabins, each holding roughly six to eight passengers, were engineered to operate reliably in sub-zero temperatures and heavy snow loading, conditions that make the Kashmir Himalaya one of the more demanding environments in which a Pomagalski system has been installed.

A defining feature of the completed system is the view it opens up at the upper station, where passengers disembark onto a windswept shoulder of Apharwat Peak with sightlines across the Kashmir valley and, on clear days, toward the distant Nanga Parbat massif. The second phase in particular proved transformative for winter sports, since it placed skiers and snowboarders at elevations previously reachable only by hiking or helicopter, catalysing Gulmarg's emergence as a base for heli-skiing operators working the bowls and glades below Apharwat. The lift has also periodically drawn attention for operational interruptions, including weather-related shutdowns and occasional security-related suspensions, given its location close to the Line of Control, with services restored under official clearance each time normal conditions resume.

Today the gondola is serviced and upgraded under the continued oversight of the Jammu & Kashmir Cable Car Corporation, which coordinates seasonal safety inspections and maintenance in partnership with technical teams associated with the original French engineering expertise. The lift moves several hundred passengers an hour between stages during peak season, supporting both the ski industry and a growing non-skiing tourist traffic that rides it simply for the high-altitude scenery. Ticketing, capacity limits, and weather-based suspensions are managed directly by the corporation, which has positioned the gondola alongside similar high-altitude lifts worldwide even as it remains firmly tied to Gulmarg's own identity as Kashmir's principal winter sports town.
