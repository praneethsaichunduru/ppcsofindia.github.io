Mohandas Gandhi Vidyalaya

મોહનદાસ ગાંધી વિદ્યાલય

Mohandas Gandhi Vidyalaya traces its origins to the Rajkot English School, founded on 17 October 1853 under the patronage of the British political agent in the princely state, making it the first English-medium school in the Saurashtra region. The institution was renamed Rajkot High School by 1868, and in 1907 it took the name Alfred High School, commemorating Prince Alfred, Duke of Edinburgh, whose visit to the region the new building was built to mark. Only after independence did the school acquire the name by which this post office locality now commonly knows it, honouring the pupil who had once walked its corridors as a boy from Porbandar newly arrived in the state capital.

The school's core significance rests on its role as the formative classroom of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, who was enrolled there from around 1880 and passed out in 1887, having spent his most impressionable adolescent years within its walls before sailing for London to train as a barrister. As Saurashtra's premier English-medium institution of its era, Alfred High School carried considerable prestige quite apart from its most famous alumnus, educating generations of Kathiawar's administrative and professional class. Its later renaming as Mohandas Gandhi High School reflected a deliberate post-independence effort to root the national Gandhian narrative in a tangible, visitable site rather than leave it confined to biography and legend.

The present building, opened in January 1875 by Sir Philip Wodehouse, then Governor of Bombay, was constructed through the patronage of the Nawab of Junagadh and other regional rulers specifically to honour Prince Alfred's tour, and its Gothic-Norman design with arched colonnades and a clock tower set it apart from the plainer government schools typical of the period. The structure functioned continuously as a working school for 164 years, an unusually long institutional life for a single-purpose colonial-era building in Gujarat, until the state government withdrew it from educational use in May 2017 to repurpose it as a heritage site.

A distinguishing episode in the site's more recent history is the period of neglect that followed its 2017 closure, when the vacated building sat largely dilapidated for roughly a year while renovation plans were finalised, prompting public concern over the condition of a structure so closely tied to Gandhi's early life. That gap ended with a large-scale restoration carried out ahead of Gandhi's 150th birth anniversary, during which the classrooms, including the one traditionally identified as Gandhi's, were refitted as exhibition galleries without erasing the building's original colonial-era fabric.

The restored complex reopened as the Mahatma Gandhi Museum, inaugurated by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 30 September 2018, and is maintained today under the Gujarat government's cultural and tourism administration as a public museum rather than a functioning school. The Mohandas Gandhi Vidyalaya name persists in local usage and on postal references such as this one precisely because the museum occupies the same building and plot, so that visitors to Rajkot can still walk into the actual rooms where Gandhi attended class well over a century ago.
