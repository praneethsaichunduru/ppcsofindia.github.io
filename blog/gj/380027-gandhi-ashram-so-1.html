Hridaykunj Sabarmati Ashram

હૃદયકુંજ સાબરમતી આશ્રમ

The name Hriday Kunj, Gujarati and Hindi for "heart's nook" or "heart's bower," was given to this cottage because it occupied the central position within the layout of Sabarmati Ashram, making it the figurative as well as literal heart of the community Gandhi assembled on the river's western bank. The ashram itself had relocated to this site on 17 June 1917, after an earlier, short-lived settlement at Kochrab proved too cramped and too exposed to a plague outbreak in the neighbourhood, and Hriday Kunj was raised soon after as the residence for Gandhi and his wife Kasturba within the new grounds.

Within the wider complex, Hriday Kunj holds the status of the ashram's principal memorial structure, since it is the one building most directly identified with Gandhi's own daily life rather than with the ashram's collective institutions such as its school, weaving sheds, or guest quarters. Gandhi and Kasturba occupied it from around 1918 until his departure on the Dandi Salt March in March 1930, and it was here that the ashram's ethic of simplicity, manual labour, and communal discipline was most visibly embodied in a single dwelling. The cottage today forms part of the protected core administered as a heritage site by the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation's heritage cell, in addition to its standing as a nationally significant Gandhian memorial.

The structure is a modest, single-storey house built in plain masonry with a tiled roof, lattice-work doors and windows, and verandahs opening onto the courtyard, consistent with the restrained construction used throughout the ashram and deliberately free of ornamentation. Its interior is divided into a small number of functional spaces, including a bedroom-cum-study, a kitchen area, a store room, and an open veranda that at times doubled as an informal secretariat where ashram correspondence and visitors were handled. Surviving furnishings include a low writing desk, a charkha, simple mud and metal utensils, and an old armchair, items retained largely as they were used rather than arranged as a conventional museum display.

A particular episode associated with the cottage is Gandhi's declaration, made as he left Hriday Kunj at dawn on 12 March 1930 to begin the Salt March to Dandi, that he would not return to the ashram until India had won its independence; he kept that resolve, and after 1930 the cottage ceased to be his permanent residence even on his later visits to Ahmedabad. In the years before that departure, the veranda and courtyard of Hriday Kunj had served as the setting for discussions with figures ranging from Sardar Patel and Jawaharlal Nehru to visiting foreign journalists and sympathisers, giving the modest cottage an outsized role in the planning of major phases of the independence movement.

Hriday Kunj is maintained today by the Sabarmati Ashram Preservation and Memorial Trust, one of five trusts that between them administer different portions of the roughly five-acre core ashram site, and it draws the largest share of visitor footfall among the complex's surviving original structures. The cottage has also figured in the Gujarat government's broader Gandhi Ashram Memorial and Precinct Development Project, a large-scale redevelopment proposal covering some 35 acres and dozens of heritage buildings, which has drawn public objections from Gandhian organisations concerned about changes to the site's original character. Pending the outcome of that redevelopment debate, Hriday Kunj remains open to the public in its long-familiar form, still visited chiefly as the room-by-room record of how Gandhi actually lived rather than as a reconstructed set piece.
