Alfred High School

આલ્ફ્રેડ હાઇસ્કૂલ

Alfred High School in Rajkot traces its origin to 17 October 1853, when it opened as the Rajkot English School with an initial enrollment of only 36 students, making it the first English-medium school established in the Saurashtra (then Kathiawar) region. By 1868 the institution had come to be known as Rajkot High School, and in 1907 it was renamed Alfred High School in honour of Prince Alfred, Duke of Edinburgh, a member of the British royal family who had toured India. The present school building was inaugurated in January 1875 by Sir Philip Wodehouse, then Governor of Bombay, cementing the institution's status as a flagship of colonial-era education in the princely states of Kathiawar.

As an educational institution, Alfred High School holds a distinctive place in the history of modern Indian schooling, having operated continuously in the same premises for 164 years, from 1853 until its closure as a functioning school in 2017. Its significance extends well beyond pedagogy, however, resting almost entirely on a single alumnus: Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, who was enrolled here (then styled Kathiawar High School) for roughly seven years and completed his matriculation in 1887 at the age of eighteen, by his own later account an unremarkable but conscientious student. This connection has made the site one of the most visited locations associated with Gandhi's early life anywhere in Gujarat, alongside his birthplace at Kirti Mandir in Porbandar.

The school's present building, distinct from its original 1853 premises, was funded by the Nawab of Junagadh, Sir Muhammad Bahadur Khanji Babi, and constructed in a Gothic-Norman colonial idiom typical of late nineteenth-century institutional architecture in western India. The structure is built of red stone with arched windows and doorways, and its classrooms are arranged around a central open courtyard reached through covered corridors, a layout designed to suit the region's climate while projecting the civic grandeur associated with British-era public schools. This architectural fabric survived largely intact through the twentieth century even as the school's academic role eventually wound down in 2017 after well over a century and a half of continuous instruction.

The defining event tied to the building's later history was its conversion into the Mahatma Gandhi Museum, undertaken by the Gujarat government and the Rajkot Municipal Corporation at a reported cost of around Rs 26 crore, and inaugurated on 30 September 2018 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi together with the state's chief minister and deputy chief minister as part of the commemorations marking the 150th birth anniversary of Gandhi. The restoration preserved the original Gothic-Norman facade and courtyard plan while converting the former classrooms into 39 themed galleries covering Gandhi's childhood, his South African Satyagraha years, and milestones of the Indian freedom struggle such as the Dandi Salt March, using holograms, 3D projection and interactive digital displays rather than static exhibits alone.

The museum is now jointly administered by the Rajkot Municipal Corporation and the Gujarat Tourism department, which run free guided tours in English, Hindi and Gujarati and stage a daily twenty-minute light-and-sound show recreating episodes from Gandhi's life using 3D technology. Visitors typically require at least an hour to move through the full sequence of galleries, and the site has since been positioned by state tourism authorities as a benchmark for immersive, technology-driven heritage museums in Gujarat. What began in 1853 as a modest English school for 36 boys in Kathiawar has thus been repurposed, without losing its colonial-era shell, into one of Rajkot's principal cultural institutions dedicated to the city's most famous student.
