The Indian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar (IITGN) traces its origin to 2008, when it was established as part of a second wave of IITs created by the Government of India to widen access to premier technical education beyond the five original institutes. It began operations from a temporary campus before relocating to its permanent site at Palaj, on the outskirts of Gandhinagar along the Sabarmati river, in Gujarat. Unlike the older IITs, which grew gradually from colonial-era engineering colleges, IITGN was conceived from its founding charter to be interdisciplinary, giving it room to build a visual and architectural identity, including its institutional logo and campus landmarks, without the constraints of a pre-existing institutional image.

As an academic brand mark, the IITGN logo functions as the institute's core identifier across degrees, publications, and official correspondence, standing alongside the physical campus landmark known as Lal Minar as the two most recognisable visual references to the institution. IITGN has built its institutional significance less on age or legacy and more on curricular distinctiveness: it mandates coursework in design, the life sciences, and the liberal arts alongside conventional engineering training, an approach that earned its five-week Foundation Programme for incoming undergraduates the World Education Award in 2013. The institute's campus has also been rated among India's highest-graded green campuses under the GRIHA rating system, tying its institutional identity closely to sustainability credentials rarely emphasised by older technical institutes.

Lal Minar, the red tower that anchors the campus skyline alongside the logo, is a tall concrete structure whose visible face carries an elaborate embossed pattern rather than plain cladding. The design was the subject of internal debate during the campus's development between those who wanted the tower's surface to directly reproduce the institute's logo and those who favoured a more universal national symbol; the resolution was an abstract pattern loosely drawn from the form of an ancient Indian sundial capable of reading time to within a minute's accuracy. The resulting embossing, cast directly into concrete at the tower's height, is held by the institute to be among the largest works of concrete embossing attempted in India at that scale, making the tower as much an engineering and craft exercise as an architectural one.

The choice of a sundial-derived motif for Lal Minar gives the tower a layered symbolism distinct from a straightforward reproduction of the institute's crest: it gestures toward ancient Indian scientific instrumentation and timekeeping precision while still reading, from a distance, as an abstract marker of the campus rather than a literal replica of any specific historical monument such as Jantar Mantar. This has made the tower a frequently photographed orientation point on campus, used informally by students and visitors as a wayfinding landmark distinct from the institute's formally registered logo, which instead appears on academic documents, campus signage, and official merchandise.

Today the logo and Lal Minar are jointly treated by the institute as public-facing symbols of its identity, referenced together in campus literature and orientation materials for new students, with the tower maintained as part of the Palaj campus's built infrastructure under IITGN's estates and facilities administration. The institute, now housing undergraduate, postgraduate, and doctoral programmes across engineering, sciences, and humanities disciplines, continues to cite both the tower and its logo in institutional communications as shorthand for the Palaj campus itself, a practice that has made the pairing a convenient visual stand-in for the institution in contexts ranging from admissions brochures to, as with this cancellation, postal philatelic material issued from the IIT Gandhinagar SO post office.
