Shri Ram Janmbhoomi Temple

श्री राम जन्मभूमि मंदिर

Ayodhya, on the banks of the Sarayu river, is venerated in Hindu tradition as the birthplace of Lord Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu and hero of the Ramayana, and the site now occupied by the Shri Ram Janmbhoomi Temple is held by believers to be the precise spot of his birth. A mosque, the Babri Masjid, had stood at the location since the sixteenth century, and the question of what structure preceded it on the site became the central matter of a dispute that ran through Indian courts and public life for over a century, culminating in the mosque's demolition by a mob in 1992. The temple that stands today is thus built on a site whose identity as Lord Rama's janmasthan long predates any contemporary construction.

The dispute over the site was settled by a unanimous Supreme Court of India judgment delivered on 9 November 2019, which awarded the disputed 2.77-acre plot for construction of a Hindu temple while directing the government to allot alternate land elsewhere in Ayodhya for a new mosque. Following the verdict, the Government of India constituted the Shri Ram Janmbhoomi Teerth Kshetra trust in February 2020 to oversee the temple's construction and management. The temple holds significance not only as a site of Vaishnavite pilgrimage but as a monument tied directly to a landmark constitutional and legal resolution, and its consecration has been treated by the Indian state and by Hindu religious institutions alike as a milestone event in the country's modern religious and political history.

The temple's design, in Nagara style architecture drawing on Gujarat-Chalukya traditions comparable to the Sun Temple at Konark, was prepared by the Ahmedabad-based architect Chandrakant Sompura, whose family has designed temples for fifteen generations, working with his son Ashish Sompura; an earlier version of the design dated to 1988, before being revised after the 2019 verdict. Civil construction was carried out by Larsen & Toubro with Tata Consulting Engineers as project management consultant, using pink sandstone from Rajasthan's Bansi Paharpur quarries and marble, without structural steel, following a bhumi pujan ceremony performed by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 5 August 2020. The completed main structure spans roughly 360 feet in length, 235 feet in width and 161 feet in height across three storeys with twelve gates, built across a temple complex of about 2.7 acres within a larger 70-acre campus.

The sanctum sanctorum houses an idol of Ram Lalla, the infant form of Lord Rama, carved from black Krishna shila stone by sculptor Arun Yogiraj, and this idol was ceremonially installed during the Pran Pratishtha consecration held on 22 January 2024, an event attended by Prime Minister Modi and telecast nationally to large public gatherings. The temple's layout incorporates five mandapas, namely the Nritya Mandap, Rang Mandap, Gudh Mandap, Kirtan Mandap and Prarthana Mandap, arranged ahead of the main shikhara, following classical Nagara temple planning principles. Within months of opening, the shrine recorded extraordinary visitor numbers, with trust officials citing tens of millions of pilgrims in its first year, making it one of independent India's most visited religious sites almost immediately after consecration.

Construction has continued in phases beyond the January 2024 consecration, with secondary shrines, boundary walls, a pilgrim facilitation centre and other campus elements scheduled for completion through 2025, under continued supervision by the Shri Ram Janmbhoomi Teerth Kshetra trust, which also manages donations and planning for the wider 70-acre precinct. The Ayodhya Development Authority and Uttar Pradesh state government have coordinated parallel infrastructure expansion in the surrounding city, including a redeveloped railway station and airport, to accommodate the pilgrim traffic the temple generates. For the postal history of the region, the temple now functions as the principal landmark served by Ayodhya's postal network, anchoring the area covered by the Ayodhya HO post office in present-day civic and religious life.
