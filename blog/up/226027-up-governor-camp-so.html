Raj Bhawan, Lucknow

राज भवन, लखनऊ

Raj Bhawan in Lucknow traces its origins to Kothi Hayat Baksh, meaning "abode of the gift of life," a mansion raised at the close of the eighteenth century for Nawab Saadat Ali Khan of Awadh. Its layout and construction were entrusted to Major General Claude Martin, the French soldier-architect whose European design sensibilities had already found favour with the Awadh court through earlier commissions in the city. Martin is said to have used the building for a time as his own residence and to garrison his guards and store arms, before it passed into the hands of the ruling establishment and, later, the colonial administration that succeeded the Nawabi state.

The building's core significance lies in its continuous institutional role rather than any single founding event. From 1921 to 1947 it served as Government House, the official residence of the Governor of the United Provinces under British rule, and after independence it was redesignated Raj Bhawan, the seat of the Governor of Uttar Pradesh. As the ceremonial and constitutional head of the state's executive, the Governor has occupied this address for over two centuries in one administrative capacity or another, making the building one of the longest continuously used gubernatorial residences in northern India and a fixture of Lucknow's institutional landscape alongside the Vidhan Bhawan and Lok Bhawan.

Architecturally, Raj Bhawan is a double-storeyed structure built in an Indo-European idiom characteristic of Claude Martin's other Lucknow works, combining European classical proportions, high verandas and lofty ceilings with features suited to the local climate. The main block contains spacious porticos, formal reception and meeting halls, and attached guest accommodation, all set within a sprawling landscaped campus reported at roughly fifty-two acres in the heart of the city. This scale of grounds, unusual for an urban administrative building, has allowed the complex to accommodate state banquets, investitures and large public functions without encroaching on the privacy of the Governor's working residence.

A defining episode in the building's modern history came in January 2026, when the Uttar Pradesh government renamed the residence from Raj Bhawan to Jan Bhavan, acting on a Union Ministry of Home Affairs directive of December 2025 that called for standardised, people-centric names for governors' residences across India. Because the state already had a Lok Bhawan housing the Chief Minister's office, the change settled on Jan Bhavan rather than the Lok Bhawan label adopted elsewhere, making Lucknow's gubernatorial seat one of the more closely watched examples of this nationwide renaming exercise and placing it briefly at the centre of political debate within the state.

The premises continue to be maintained by the Uttar Pradesh Public Works Department as a functioning seat of constitutional office, hosting the swearing-in of chief ministers, credential ceremonies, and official receptions much as it did under the Lieutenant Governors of the United Provinces. Postal recognition of the building, including its depiction on philatelic material issued for Raj Bhawans across the country, has helped document this older name even as the signage and official records at the Lucknow campus now read Jan Bhavan, leaving the site's two names, old and new, to mark the same continuous institution.
