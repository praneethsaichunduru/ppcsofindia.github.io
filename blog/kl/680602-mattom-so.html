Shrine of Our Lady of Perpetual Help

അമ്മയുടെ ശാശ്വത സഹായത്തിന്റെ ദേവാലയം

The Shrine of Our Lady of Perpetual Help at Mattom owes its origin to Fr Sebastian Vellanikkaran, a priest of the Syro-Malabar Church who, after a visit to Rome, brought back a copy of the venerated picture of Our Lady of Perpetual Help and installed it in a niche carved into a rock on a hillock at Mattom. The image was formally blessed in 1938, an act that is locally regarded as the founding moment of the shrine, and the hillock location itself has since lent the site a quiet, elevated setting distinct from the parish churches that surround it in this part of Thalapilly taluk.

The devotion reproduced at Mattom traces back to a much older Marian icon, painted in the Byzantine style and traditionally dated to between the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, which was venerated for centuries on the island of Crete before being carried to Rome around 1499 and installed in the Augustinian church of San Matteo. The Mattom shrine functions today as a Syro-Malabar Catholic pilgrimage site under the Metropolitan Archdiocese of Trichur, carrying the devotional and institutional weight associated with its sponsoring picture while serving a strictly local and regional body of devotees rather than functioning as a parish church in its own right.

After the original icon passed into the custody of the Redemptorist congregation and was enshrined above the high altar of the Church of Sant'Alfonso on Rome's Via Merulana in 1866, devotion to Our Lady of Perpetual Help spread rapidly through copies distributed to churches worldwide, of which the Mattom image is one example. At Mattom, the copy was not placed inside a built sanctuary at the outset but set into the natural rock of the hillock, a simple and distinctly site-specific arrangement that has since been built upon with chapel structures to accommodate the pilgrims and worshippers who gather there, particularly during the shrine's annual devotional season.

A defining feature of the Mattom shrine is its annual novena, the nine-day cycle of prayer that precedes the principal feast and draws its largest congregations of the year, mirroring the novena tradition that has been closely associated with Our Lady of Perpetual Help devotion since its revival at the Redemptorist church in Rome in the nineteenth century. During this period the hillock shrine, ordinarily a site of modest daily visitation, becomes the focus of organised processions and extended services that bring together devotees from Mattom's own St Thomas Forane parish community as well as from neighbouring parishes across Thrissur district.

The shrine sits within a locality whose Christian associations run deep, as the nearby St Thomas Forane Church at Mattom is reckoned among the historic Syro-Malabar parishes of central Kerala with a founding tradition reaching back many centuries, giving the hillock devotion an established Christian setting to grow within rather than standing in isolation. Administered within the pastoral structure of the Archdiocese of Trichur, the shrine today continues as a site of regular visitation and annual festival gathering, maintained by local clergy and laity whose care keeps the hillside chapel and its rock-set image accessible to pilgrims throughout the year.
