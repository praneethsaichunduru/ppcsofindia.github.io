Idukki Dam

ഇടുക്കി അണക്കെട്ട്

The Idukki Dam is a concrete arch dam on the Periyar river in the Idukki district of Keralam. It closes a narrow gorge between two hills, Kuravan Mala, at about 839 metres, and Kurathi Mala, at about 925 metres. With a height of about 169 metres it is among the tallest arch dams in Asia, and it is built in the double-curvature form, curving both across the gorge and from top to bottom so that the force of the water is carried into the rock of the two hillsides.

The dam is the centre of the Idukki hydroelectric project of the Kerala State Electricity Board. The reservoir is formed by three dams, the Idukki arch dam and the Cheruthoni and Kulamavu dams, and the water is carried through tunnels to an underground power station at Moolamattom, one of the largest in the state. The arch dam has no gates of its own, and the level of the reservoir is controlled by the shutters of the Cheruthoni dam.

Construction began in 1969 with technical and financial assistance from Canada, and the reservoir began to fill in 1973. The first generating unit was tested in 1975, and the power station was commissioned in the following years. Local tradition links the discovery of the gorge to a tribal chief named Kolumban, who is said to have shown the site to a British surveyor in the 1920s, and the two hills that frame the dam are remembered in the story of Kuravan and Kurathi, a tribal couple.

Idukki Colony is the settlement near the dam that grew up around the project, with housing for the engineers and staff who built and now operate it. The Idukki Colony Sub Post Office serves this community and the visitors who come to see the dam, which is opened to the public at limited times of the year, so visitors should check the timings before they travel.

The pictorial cancellation shows the dam that has become a symbol of the development of Keralam's hills. For collectors, it recalls an engineering project that changed the economy of the state and the landscape of the high range.
