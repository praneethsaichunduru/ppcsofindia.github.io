The emblem of the Mata Amritanandamayi Math is a composite devotional symbol rather than a mere corporate insignia, built around the image of a lotus rising out of water beneath a sun, with a conch placed alongside. Each element carries a specific meaning within the organisation's own stated philosophy: the lotus leaves stand for the mind and intellect, the lotus flower itself for devotion (bhakti), the water for selfless action (karma), the sun for knowledge (jnana), and the conch for the primordial sacred sound Aum. Read together, the logo is meant to depict how inner purification achieved through selfless action, guided by mind and intellect, allows the lotus of devotion to blossom and knowledge to arise, uniting jnana, bhakti, and karma in one emblem.

The Mata Amritanandamayi Math (MAM) is the registered spiritual and charitable trust that institutionally anchors the global mission popularly known through its founder, Sudhamani Idamannel (born 27 September 1953 in the fishing hamlet of Parayakadavu), who came to be revered as Mata Amritanandamayi or simply 'Amma'. The organisation was formally constituted on 6 May 1981 at Amritapuri, in Alappad panchayat of Kollam district, Kerala, and has since grown into one of India's most prominent faith-based humanitarian networks, classified under Hindu-derived new religious and charitable movements. Its significance rests less on temple ritual than on an institutionalised ethic of seva (selfless service), expressed through disaster relief, free healthcare, housing, and education programmes carried out under the Math's banner across India and abroad.

Amritapuri itself evolved from the modest coastal hut where Amma conducted her early darshan sessions into a sprawling residential and administrative campus that now houses several thousand permanent residents, monastics, and visiting devotees, with high-rise residential blocks constructed to accommodate the expanding community on the narrow spit of land between backwaters and sea. The Math's logo, displayed on its publications, gateways, and institutional signage, visually anchors this campus and its affiliated bodies, including the Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham university system, Amrita Institute of Medical Sciences in Kochi, and the AMMACHI Labs technology-for-development initiative, all operating under trusts linked to the central Math.

A defining feature associated with the Math and its emblem is the scale of Amma's public embrace programme, through which she is reported to have personally hugged more than thirty-four million people across decades of darshan tours in India and dozens of other countries, a practice the Math cites as the living expression of the compassion represented by the lotus and water in its logo. The organisation has also been the subject of independent scrutiny and occasional controversy over its finances and internal governance, which the Math has addressed through published audited accounts and statements defending its charitable record, including large-scale disaster response after the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami and subsequent Kerala floods.

Today the Mata Amritanandamayi Math functions as the parent charitable trust overseeing Embracing the World, the umbrella coordinating MAM's international humanitarian projects, alongside its Amritapuri headquarters, which remains the administrative and spiritual nerve centre for the Math's hospitals, schools, and relief operations. The emblem, reproduced on the special postal pictorial cancellation issued for Amrithapuri Sub Office in Kollam district, gives philatelists a compact visual record of the devotional and humanitarian synthesis that the institution has built around Amma's teaching over more than four decades.
