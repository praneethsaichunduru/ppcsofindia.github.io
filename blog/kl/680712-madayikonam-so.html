Lighted Candles in Holy Cross Church, Mapranam

മാപ്രാണം വിശുദ്ധ കുരിശുപള്ളിയിലെ തിരി വിളക്കുകൾ

The Holy Cross Church at Mapranam, near Madayikonam in Thrissur district, traces its founding to around 928 CE, making it one of the oldest Christian places of worship in Kerala. Local tradition holds that the church came to house a fragment of the True Cross, a relic of the Holy Blood of Christ, and a piece of the cloth attributed to Veronica, all gifted from the Vatican during the pontificate of Pope Leo XIII, with the formal installation of the Holy Cross relic recorded on 21 September 1887. This association with a Vatican-sanctioned relic set the church apart from the many other Kerala parishes dedicated to the Holy Cross, giving Mapranam a distinct identity as a shrine built around a tangible object of veneration rather than devotion to the symbol alone.

Administered under the Syro-Malabar Catholic Diocese of Irinjalakuda, the Mar Sleeva Church at Mapranam was elevated to the status of a shrine in 2008 and has also been designated a Historical Pilgrim Centre by the Government of Kerala. Its significance rests on three overlapping strands: the antiquity of its founding among the first wave of Christian communities tracing descent from the Apostle Thomas's arrival in 52 CE, the rarity of its Vatican-origin relics, and its emergence as a pilgrimage destination whose annual feast now draws worshippers from across the state. This combination of relic-based sanctity, ecclesiastical recognition, and state heritage status distinguishes Mapranam from parish churches whose importance is primarily local or purely devotional.

The present church structure was reconstructed in 1989, replacing earlier buildings that had served the congregation since the medieval period, and it continues to function as an active parish church within the Irinjalakuda diocese. Its principal observance, the feast of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross, is centred on 14 September, with the main celebrations spanning 12 to 15 September and an octave observed on 21 September, echoing the date of the original relic installation. The defining ritual of this feast period is Thirithelikkal, the offering of candles, performed on the evening of 13 September at the adjoining St John's Chapel, locally called Kurisu Kappela, where several hundred devotees bring candles ranging from modest sizes up to several hundred kilograms in weight as acts of vow-fulfilment and thanksgiving.

The most striking expression of this candle-offering tradition occurred on 31 August 2007, when a single candle weighing 1,079 kilograms, standing 22 feet tall with an 18-inch diameter, was ceremonially installed at the church; the candle was subsequently entered into the Limca Book of Records as the largest in India. Such outsized votive candles, often commissioned by individual families or groups in fulfilment of personal vows, are carried in procession before being lit and placed within the chapel, drawing large crowds of onlookers alongside the devotees making the offering. The scale of these candles has become the church's most recognisable feature, turning what elsewhere in Kerala remains a modest devotional gesture into a public spectacle unique to Mapranam.

The feast continues to be organised jointly by the parish and diocesan authorities, with the eve-of-feast candle offering remaining the single largest draw for pilgrims and visitors from across Thrissur district and beyond. The shrine's dual recognition, as a diocesan pilgrim centre and as a state-listed historical site, has brought continued attention to its upkeep and to the conduct of its annual celebrations, which the Postal Department has marked with a permanent pictorial cancellation at the Madayikonam Sub Office. The cancellation gives philatelic recognition to a devotional practice that, for the parish itself, remains simply the yearly renewal of a vow-offering tradition carried on by successive generations of its congregation.
