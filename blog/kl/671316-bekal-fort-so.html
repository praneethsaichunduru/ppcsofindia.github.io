Bekal Fort

ബേക്കൽ കോട്ട

The name Bekal is widely held to derive from the Malayalam "Baliakulam" or "Baliakotta," meaning the great village or great fortification, a reference to the site's long-standing importance as a coastal settlement well before the present fort was raised. Local tradition and inscriptional references also point to earlier mud and timber fortifications on the same promontory under regional Tuluva and Kolathiri chieftains, which the Keladi Nayakas later superseded with a permanent laterite structure. The present fort's construction is generally dated to around 1650, begun under Hiriya Venkatappa Nayaka and completed by Sivappa Nayaka, placing Bekal within a lineage of coastal strongholds built to secure the pepper and spice trade routes along the Malabar coast.

Spread across roughly 40 acres on a seaward promontory, Bekal is recognised as the largest fort in Kerala and one of the best preserved examples of medieval military architecture on the Malabar coast. Its significance lies less in religious or ceremonial function and more in its strategic and historical role, having passed successively from the Keladi Nayakas of Karnataka to the Mysore Sultanate under Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, and finally to the British East India Company after the Third Anglo-Mysore War in the late eighteenth century. This layered ownership makes the fort a physical record of the shifting regional powers that contested control of Kerala's northern coast, and it is maintained today as a centrally protected monument under the Archaeological Survey of India.

Built almost entirely of laterite blocks quarried locally, the fort follows a distinctive keyhole-shaped outline when viewed in plan, with thick curtain walls rising directly from the rocky shoreline and bastions positioned to cover approaches from both land and sea. Tunnels connect various points within the fort, including a passage opening toward the south that may have served as an escape route or a concealed approach to the water's edge, while wide, gently inclined steps lead up to a central observation tower unusual for the breadth of its stairway. During Tipu Sultan's period of control, additional features were incorporated, including a stepped water tank, an ammunition magazine, and a small mosque, layered onto the earlier Nayaka-era fabric and reflecting the fort's continued military use under successive rulers.

The observation tower at the fort's highest point is among its most distinguishing elements, commanding a sweeping view that takes in Bekal beach, the Arabian Sea, and the neighbouring towns of Kanhangad, Pallikkara, Mavval, Kottikkulam, and Uduma, a vantage that made the fort valuable for monitoring both naval movement and coastal trade. Close to the fort stands an old mosque alongside a Hanuman temple, a juxtaposition locally cited as evidence of the religious coexistence that shaped the area's social history. The fort gained wider popular recognition in 1995 when director Mani Ratnam filmed a song sequence for his film Bombay on its ramparts and the rocky outcrops below, a sequence that introduced the site to audiences well beyond Kerala and has since drawn numerous other film and television productions to the location.

In 1992 the Government of India designated Bekal a special tourist area, and in 1995 the Bekal Resorts Development Corporation was set up to oversee planned tourism development around the fort and its adjoining beaches, including the nearby Kappil and Pallikere stretches. The Archaeological Survey of India has since carried out conservation work on the walls and tunnels and laid out visitor pathways that allow access along the ramparts without further strain on the laterite masonry, while the state tourism department runs a small park and children's attractions just outside the fort walls. Managed jointly by these two bodies, Bekal Fort now functions as both a protected archaeological site and one of Kasaragod district's principal visitor draws, drawing a steady stream of tourists to walk its ramparts and photograph the coastline from its tower.
