Mysuru: Our Heritage - Your Destination

ಮೈಸೂರು: ನಮ್ಮ ಪರಂಪರೆ - ನಿಮ್ಮ ತಾಣ

The phrase "Mysuru: Our Heritage, Your Destination" is a tourism tagline developed under Karnataka's tourism establishment and the Mysuru district administration as part of a wider "Brand Mysuru" push to give the city its own promotional identity distinct from the state's broader campaigns. Rather than naming a monument or place, the slogan functions as a civic brand, pairing the possessive "our" (the custodianship Mysuru and Karnataka claim over the former princely capital's royal past) with "your" (an invitation addressed directly to the traveller), a device typical of destination marketing aimed at converting inherited history into a reason to visit.

The tagline's core identity rests on Mysuru's standing as the former capital of the Kingdom of Mysore under the Wadiyar dynasty, one of India's longest-ruling royal houses, and on the city's present-day position as Karnataka's principal heritage and cultural tourism hub after Bengaluru. Its claim to "heritage" is reinforced by concrete recognitions rather than general sentiment: Mysuru Dasara, the ten-day festival observed in its royal form under the Wadiyars, is designated Karnataka's official state festival (nadahabba), and products associated with the city, including Mysore Silk, Mysore Sandal soap, and Mysore Pak, hold Geographical Indication tags that formally protect their regional identity.

The physical centrepiece around which the branding is built is the Mysore Palace, also called Amba Vilas Palace, rebuilt between 1897 and 1912 after fire destroyed the earlier wooden structure, under commission from Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV. Its design is credited to British architect Henry Irwin, who had earlier designed the Madras High Court, with later work by Edwin Fritchley, and the finished building blends Indo-Saracenic, Rajput, Hindu, and Gothic elements into a structure counted among India's most-visited monuments. Successive Wadiyar rulers extended this civic imprint beyond the palace itself, funding markets, gardens, educational institutions, and the layout of planned residential localities across the city, including the University of Mysore, one of Karnataka's oldest universities.

A distinguishing feature of the brand is Chamundi Hill, overlooking the city, where the Chamundeshwari Temple sits atop roughly a thousand stone steps and draws pilgrims and sightseers alike, giving the slogan a devotional dimension alongside its courtly one. Mysuru has also built a separate reputation as a global centre for Ashtanga yoga, drawing practitioners from abroad for extended study, a strand of "destination" appeal that sits apart from the palace-and-festival narrative and broadens the tagline's reach beyond conventional heritage tourism into wellness travel.

Present-day stewardship of this branding effort involves the Mysuru district administration and the Karnataka Department of Tourism, who have periodically invited public competitions to refine the city's logo and messaging, treating the resulting intellectual property as official civic branding rather than an informal nickname. The Mysore Palace itself remains under the joint custody of the former royal family's private trust and state authorities, who continue to manage its public access, ticketed visits, and the nightly illumination that draws crowds on weekends and during Dasara. Taken together, the tagline endures less as a slogan fixed in place than as a working label the city's tourism machinery keeps returning to and updating around the same cluster of palace, festival, hill shrine, and craft traditions.
