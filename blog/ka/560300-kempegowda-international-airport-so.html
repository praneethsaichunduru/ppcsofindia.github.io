Statue of Prosperity & Kempegowda International Airport

The Statue of Prosperity is a monumental bronze sculpture of Nadaprabhu Kempe Gowda I, the sixteenth-century Vijayanagara feudatory chieftain credited with founding the city of Bengaluru in 1537, installed at the city's international airport in Devanahalli. The statue depicts Kempe Gowda in a standing pose bearing a sword, commemorating the founder-ruler whose vision for a fortified settlement with tanks, markets and temple precincts shaped the layout of what grew into modern Bengaluru. Conceived as a landmark greeting arriving and departing passengers, it ties the airport's identity directly to the city's founding figure rather than to a generic civic emblem.

Standing 108 feet tall and weighing approximately 218 tonnes, including a sword component alone weighing close to four tonnes, the statue ranks among the tallest bronze statues of a historical city founder anywhere, a distinction recognised by the World Book of Records. It was sculpted by Padma Bhushan awardee Ram Vanji Sutar, the veteran artist also known for India's Statue of Unity, using roughly ninety-eight tonnes of bronze over a steel-and-concrete structural core of about one hundred twenty tonnes. The work forms the centrepiece of a broader commemorative project undertaken by the Karnataka government and Bengaluru International Airport Limited, intended to honour Kempe Gowda's founding legacy at the gateway most visitors use to enter the city.

Fabrication was carried out over roughly two years, with sections of the statue cast and assembled at facilities near Noida before transport to Bengaluru for final erection on site at the airport. The completed figure was unveiled by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 11 November 2022, alongside the inauguration of the airport's Terminal 2, in the presence of the then chief minister of Karnataka, marking both the statue's dedication and a new phase of expansion for the airport itself. The statue sits atop a raised plinth that lifts its visible profile well above the surrounding approach road, making it visible to arriving traffic well before travellers reach the terminal buildings.

The statue is set within a larger heritage theme park spanning approximately twenty-three acres, developed alongside it at a combined project cost reported at around eighty-four crore rupees, intended to give visitors a dedicated space to learn about Kempe Gowda's life and the founding of Bengaluru rather than encountering the statue as an isolated roadside monument. This pairing of a monumental public sculpture with an interpretive park reflects a pattern increasingly seen at major Indian airports, where large-scale commemorative art is used to assert a distinct regional identity for travellers passing through. Terminal 2 itself, opened in phases from late 2022, was designed around a garden theme with extensive green walls and landscaping, into which the statue and its park were integrated as a unified arrival experience.

Kempegowda International Airport, originally opened in 2008 to replace the older HAL Airport and renamed in 2013 in honour of the city's founder, has since grown into one of India's busiest aviation hubs, serving as the principal gateway to Bengaluru's technology and aerospace industries. The Statue of Prosperity and its adjoining park are maintained as part of the airport operator's public infrastructure, with the statue functioning as a de facto orientation point and photo landmark for passengers moving between the terminal and ground transport. Its scale and placement have made it one of the first physical impressions visitors form of Bengaluru, well before they reach any other part of the city.
