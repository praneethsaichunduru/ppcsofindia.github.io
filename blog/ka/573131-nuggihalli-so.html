Sri Hoysala Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy Temple

ಶ್ರೀ ಹೊಯ್ಸಳ ಲಕ್ಷ್ಮೀ ನರಸಿಂಹ ಸ್ವಾಮಿ ದೇವಸ್ಥಾನ

The Lakshmi Narasimha temple at Nuggehalli stands in a settlement that, prior to the 14th century, was known as Vijaya Somanathapura, a name later supplanted by the village's modern form. The locality rose to prominence as an agrahara, a grant settlement endowed for Brahmin scholars and Vedic learning, during the period of the Hoysala official who commissioned the temple, and this institutional role as a centre of learning shaped the village's identity alongside its religious functions. The present name, Nuggehalli, is the one carried by the post office and the surrounding taluk in Hassan district today.

The temple is a Vaishnava shrine complex of considerable importance within the corpus of Hoysala religious architecture, and it is often cited alongside the nearby Sadashiva temple in the same village as evidence of Nuggehalli's status as a twin temple town under Hoysala patronage. Dedicated jointly to three forms of Vishnu, it exemplifies the trikuta, or triple-shrine, format that the Hoysalas favoured for complex devotional programmes, and it is valued today both as a functioning place of worship and as a protected monument of historical and art-historical significance illustrating the empire's architectural idiom in its later phase.

Construction is attributed to 1246 CE, commissioned by Bommanna Dandanayaka, a military commander and administrator serving under the Hoysala king Vira Someshwara. The temple is built on a raised plinth, or jagati, a hallmark of Hoysala design that allows devotees to circumambulate the shrine at an elevated level, and it comprises three shrines, each crowned with its own tower, dedicated respectively to Kezhava, Lakshmi Narasimha, and Venugopala. The outer walls carry extensive figural friezes depicting Vaishnava, Shaiva, and Shakta deities, including Harihara, Dakshinamurti, Chandikeshvara, Ganesha, and Durga Mahishasuramardini, alongside Vedic figures such as Surya and Brahma, with narrative panels below drawn from the Hindu epics and the Bhagavata Purana.

A particularly notable feature of the temple is the body of sculptural work signed by Mallitamma, one of the few named artisans whose signed reliefs survive from the Hoysala period, making the Nuggehalli temple an important reference point for scholars tracing individual craftsmen's contributions within an architectural tradition otherwise largely anonymous. The dancing forms of Lakshmi and Saraswati rendered on the temple walls are frequently highlighted in art-historical surveys of Hoysala sculpture for their fluidity and detail, distinguishing this shrine's carving programme from more generic devotional imagery found elsewhere.

The temple falls under the protection of the Karnataka state department of archaeology and museums as a monument of historical importance, and it is situated on the Tiptur-Channarayapatna state highway, roughly 50 kilometres from Hassan city, making it accessible to visitors travelling the Hoysala temple circuit that also includes Belur, Halebidu, and Somanathapura. It is this combination of a named Hoysala master craftsman's work and the village's lesser-travelled setting that keeps the Nuggehalli temple a point of interest for specialists and temple architecture enthusiasts even as larger sites draw the greater share of visitor traffic.
