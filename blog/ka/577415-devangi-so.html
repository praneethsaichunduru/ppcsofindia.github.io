Kavi Shaila

ಕವಿಶೈಲ

Kavishaila, meaning 'poet's rock' in Kannada, is the name given to a rounded granite hillock rising above the village of Kuppalli in the Tirthahalli taluk of Shivamogga district, standing just beside the ancestral home of Kuvempu (K. V. Puttappa, 1904-1994). The name was not an ancient toponym but one that attached itself to the outcrop only after it became linked with the poet, reflecting the Malnad custom of naming a landscape feature after the literary or spiritual figure who gave it meaning, much as the hillock had earlier served as an informal gathering spot where Kuvempu and fellow writers sat to talk.

Kavishaila belongs to the category of literary memorial sites, and its significance rests entirely on its association with Kuvempu, the first Kannada writer honoured with the Jnanpith Award, which he received in 1967 for his epic retelling of the Ramayana, 'Sri Ramayana Darshanam.' Kuvempu was also conferred the Padma Bhushan and Padma Vibhushan and composed 'Jaya Bharata Jananiya Tanujate,' which was adopted as Karnataka's state anthem (Nadageete). Because the site marks the spot where he was cremated in keeping with his own wishes, Kavishaila functions as a de facto samadhi, placing it among the small number of Kannada literary pilgrimage destinations alongside the poet's birthplace itself.

The memorial at Kavishaila takes the form of a cluster of large, naturally rounded granite boulders arranged on the hilltop in a manner that observers have likened informally to a megalithic circle, with the central space marking the cremation site now built up into a low memorial platform. Granite slabs set into the surrounding rock face carry engraved lines from Kuvempu's poetry and quotations attributed to him, placed there as part of the site's development after his death in 1994; the adjoining family home, a traditional three-storeyed Malnad tiled house known locally as a 'thotti mane' for its open central courtyard, has separately been converted into the Kavimane museum under the Rashtrakavi Kuvempu Pratishtana, the trust established in 1992 to manage his legacy.

A distinctive feature of the hillock is a particular boulder bearing etched signatures of Kuvempu together with those of fellow Kannada literary figures B. M. Srikantaiah and T. S. Venkannaiah, commemorating the informal literary discussions once held at that spot; the writer and naturalist Poornachandra Tejaswi, Kuvempu's son, later added his own signature to the same rock, extending the marker across a second generation of the family's literary contribution. This signed boulder, distinct from the central memorial platform, is pointed out to visitors as the most tangible trace of the gatherings that gave the hillock its early, informal identity as a meeting place before it became a memorial.

In more recent years the site has drawn attention beyond conventional tourism: a research initiative by the digital-heritage group Axesmap produced a three-dimensional digitised reconstruction of the Kavishaila memorial and its etched signature rock, intended to document the site's stone carvings and layout for conservation and study. The hillock and the neighbouring Kavimane museum are maintained together by the Rashtrakavi Kuvempu Pratishtana as a single heritage circuit in Kuppalli, drawing Kannada readers, students, and literary scholars who visit the memorial as part of a wider itinerary through the Malnad countryside that shaped Kuvempu's writing.
