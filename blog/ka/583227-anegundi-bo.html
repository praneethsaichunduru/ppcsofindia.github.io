Kishkinda: Anjanadri Hill

ಕಿಷ್ಕಿಂಧಾ: ಅಂಜನಾದ್ರಿ ಬೆಟ್ಟ

According to the Ramayana, the monkey kingdom of Kishkindha was ruled in succession by the brothers Vali and Sugriva, and it was here that Hanuman, son of the wind god Vayu and the celestial being Anjana, was born on the hill that came to bear his mother's name, Anjanadri or Anjaneya Parvata. Local and scholarly tradition since at least the Vijayanagara period has identified this Kishkindha with the rocky terrain flanking the Tungabhadra river around Anegundi and Hampi, in present-day Koppal district, a identification reinforced by the area's older name and its continuing association with Hanuman worship.

Anjanadri Hill is today counted among the most visited Hanuman pilgrimage sites in Karnataka, drawing devotees who regard it as the deity's literal birthplace rather than merely a symbolic shrine. Its significance is heightened by its proximity to the Hampi group of monuments, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, so that visitors to the ruined Vijayanagara capital frequently cross the river to Anegundi to combine archaeological tourism with devotional pilgrimage, giving the hill a dual identity as both a mythological landmark and an adjunct stop on the Hampi heritage circuit.

The hill rises above Hanumanahalli village on the northern bank of the Tungabhadra, opposite Hampi, and is reached by a flight commonly cited as around 570 to 575 stone steps cut into the granite slope. At the summit stands a modest whitewashed temple enshrining a rock-cut image of Hanuman, alongside subsidiary shrines to Rama, Sita, and Anjana herself; the climb, though steep, is used daily by pilgrims and is punctuated by resting points and vendors along the route, with the surrounding landscape of boulder-strewn hills and paddy fields visible for miles from the top.

The hill draws its largest crowds on Saturdays, the day of the week traditionally associated with Hanuman worship, when queues can stretch for hours and the temple trust organizes additional arrangements for darshan and prasad distribution. The site has also benefited indirectly from the broader Kishkindha tourism circuit that has grown around Anegundi in recent decades, which includes coracle crossings of the Tungabhadra, visits to the Pampa Sarovar lake associated with Sita's search, and the ruins of the older Anegundi fort that predates the Vijayanagara empire itself.

Administration of the hill and its temple falls under local religious trusts working alongside the Karnataka state tourism and archaeology departments, which have widened approach roads and improved step maintenance to accommodate the rising pilgrim traffic, particularly since Hampi's UNESCO listing increased footfall across the region from the 1980s onward. Anegundi village itself, older than Hampi and once a refuge for the Vijayanagara royal court after the empire's fall in 1565, retains its own fort remains and the Gagan Mahal palace, so that Anjanadri Hill now functions as one node within a wider network of mythological and historical sites that pilgrims and tourists visit together along the Tungabhadra's northern bank.
