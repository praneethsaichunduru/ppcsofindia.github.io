Gangotri Temple & Gomukh

गंगोत्री मंदिर एवं गोमुख

Gangotri, at about 3,100 metres on the banks of the Bhagirathi in Uttarkashi district, is one of the four shrines of the Chota Char Dham circuit of Uttarakhand, along with Yamunotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath. It is dedicated to the goddess Ganga. According to tradition, the sage-king Bhagirath performed severe penance here to bring the Ganga down from heaven so that the ashes of his ancestors could be released, and Shiva caught the descending river in his matted locks to soften its fall.

The present white granite temple is credited to the Gorkha commander Amar Singh Thapa, from the period of Gorkha rule over Garhwal in the early nineteenth century, although some popular accounts place it in the eighteenth. It was later renovated by the royal family of Jaipur in the late nineteenth century. The temple stands close to the Bhagirath Shila, the rock on which the king is said to have meditated, and to a natural rock Shivling in the river that is visible only when the water is low in the colder months.

Gangotri follows the rhythm of the seasons. The temple closes around Diwali, and the image of the goddess is carried in a procession to Mukhba village near Harsil, where she is worshipped through the winter. The temple reopens on Akshaya Tritiya in spring, and the pilgrimage season runs until the temple closes again. Priests from local families conduct the daily rituals, and a seasonal post office serves the pilgrims who arrive during these months.

Gomukh, the snout of the Gangotri Glacier, lies about 18 to 19 kilometres upstream and is reached on foot. The glacier gets its name from the shape of the ice cave from which the Bhagirathi emerges, said to resemble a cow's mouth. The river is known as the Bhagirathi until it meets the Alaknanda at Devprayag, where the combined river takes the name Ganga. The glacier lies within Gangotri National Park, which requires permits for the trek and limits the number of visitors, and it has been retreating over the decades like many Himalayan glaciers.

The pictorial cancellation brings together the temple and the glacier, the place where the river is worshipped and the place where it begins. Together with the Badrinath cancellation of the Chota Char Dham circuit, it gives collectors a postal souvenir of the pilgrimage in the high Himalaya.
