Prakhar Pragya - Sajal Shraddha

प्रखर प्रज्ञा - सजल श्रद्धा

The phrase 'Prakhar Pragya - Sajal Shraddha' takes physical form at Shantikunj as a pair of small marble shrines flanking the ashram's main entrance, each inscribed with the footprints of its founders and named for the two qualities the movement holds inseparable: prakhar pragya, a sharp and questioning intellect, and sajal shraddha, a devotion moist with feeling rather than dry ritualism. The twin names were chosen by Pandit Shriram Sharma Acharya himself to summarise the philosophy he wished every visitor to internalise before stepping further into the campus, turning what might elsewhere be a mere gateway marker into a condensed statement of doctrine.

Shantikunj belongs to the category of modern reformist spiritual institutions rather than an ancient temple town site, and its significance rests on its role as the administrative and training headquarters of the All World Gayatri Pariwar, one of India's most widely spread socio-spiritual movements. The organisation traces its intellectual roots to a mantra-revival effort Acharya began in the 1940s, built around the Gayatri Mantra, before consolidating its institutional base at Haridwar. The complex is recognised less for monumental antiquity than for its scale of outreach, coordinating affiliated shakhas, study circles, and training centres across India and among the Indian diaspora abroad.

The ashram was established in 1971 by Pandit Shriram Sharma Acharya together with his spiritual consort Bhagwati Devi Sharma, known within the movement as Mata Bhagwati Devi, on a modest plot of land that subsequently expanded into the present Gayatri Nagar campus. Over the following decades the site grew to include a principal Gayatri temple, residential and training blocks for participants attending yoga, yagya, and value-education camps, and dedicated research and publication wings. In 1979 the founder established the Brahmvarchas Shodh Sansthan on adjoining land, a research institute intended to examine yoga, meditation, and ritual practice through a scientific lens, reflecting the twin emphasis on intellect and devotion embedded in the Prakhar Pragya-Sajal Shraddha pairing.

A distinguishing feature of the entrance shrines is their deliberately unadorned construction: rather than elaborate carving, each houses only the marble footprints of the founders, a design choice consistent with the movement's stated rejection of ostentatious ritualism in favour of simplicity and inner discipline. Pilgrims and trainees customarily pause at both shrines before proceeding into the ashram for camps or ceremonies, a practice that has become part of the standard visitor protocol at Shantikunj and is cited in devotional literature as the symbolic threshold between ordinary life and the ashram's disciplined routine of early-morning yagyas and study sessions.

Today Shantikunj functions under the continued stewardship of the All World Gayatri Pariwar's trust structure, which reports hosting hundreds of thousands of participants annually across its de-addiction drives, environmental campaigns, and Gayatri Mantra training camps, with the Prakhar Pragya-Sajal Shraddha shrines maintained as a fixed stop on the ashram's guided pilgrim circuit alongside the main temple and the Brahmvarchas Shodh Sansthan research campus. The twin shrines are thus treated less as historical relics than as a living instructional device, reintroduced to each fresh batch of trainees as the movement's shorthand for balancing reasoned enquiry with heartfelt faith.
