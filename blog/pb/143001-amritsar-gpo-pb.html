Sri Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple)

ਸ੍ਰੀ ਹਰਿਮੰਦਰ ਸਾਹਿਬ (ਸੁਨਹਿਰੀ ਮੰਦਰ)

Sikh tradition holds that the site of Sri Harmandir Sahib was chosen by the third Guru, Amar Das, and developed by the fourth Guru, Ram Das, who in 1577 began excavating the sacred pool that gives the city of Amritsar its name, Amrit Sarovar, the "pool of nectar." The foundation stone of the temple itself is traditionally said to have been laid in 1588 by the Sufi saint Mian Mir of Lahore at the invitation of the fifth Guru, Arjan Dev, an episode widely cited as a gesture of interfaith harmony, though some later Sikh scholarship has questioned the historicity of this specific account and holds that Guru Arjan may have laid it himself. The name Harmandir Sahib translates roughly as "abode of God," while the popular English name, Golden Temple, derives from its gilded upper storeys.

As the central gurdwara of Sikhism, Sri Harmandir Sahib functions as the faith's preeminent spiritual and cultural seat, comparable in stature to the holiest sites of other world religions, and draws an estimated 100,000 or more pilgrims and visitors daily, making it one of the most visited religious sites in the world. It houses the Adi Granth, the primary scripture of Sikhism, which Guru Arjan Dev compiled and installed inside the completed shrine in 1604, with Baba Buddha appointed as its first granthi. The temple complex also adjoins the Akal Takht, the temporal seat of Sikh authority facing the shrine across the sarovar, together forming the religious and administrative core of the Sikh Panth.

Construction of the temple proper was completed under Guru Arjan Dev by the early 1600s, with the building deliberately set at a lower level than the surrounding courtyard, a design choice interpreted as an expression of humility, and given four entrances on each of its sides so that people of any caste or creed could enter. The structure seen today owes much of its present form to Maharaja Ranjit Singh, founder of the Sikh Empire, who in the 1830s ordered the sanctum's upper floors and dome to be overlaid with gold, using close to 162 kilograms of the metal at the time; a further restoration between 1995 and 1999 added roughly 500 kilograms of 24-karat gold, bringing the cumulative amount applied to the shrine to around 750 kilograms.

The temple's Guru Ka Langar, the community kitchen operating continuously beside the shrine, is considered the largest free kitchen of its kind anywhere, serving a simple vegetarian meal to everyone regardless of religion, caste, or background; on an average day around 500 volunteer sevadars cook for and serve between 75,000 and 100,000 people, a figure that rises well beyond that on weekends and Sikh festivals such as Baisakhi and the birth anniversary of Guru Nanak. In June 1984, the complex was the site of Operation Blue Star, an Indian Army action against militants sheltering inside that caused severe damage to the Akal Takht; the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee has since rebuilt the structure while deliberately preserving certain bullet-scarred sections of the complex as a record of that event.

The shrine and its precincts, including the sarovar, the Akal Takht, and the Central Sikh Museum housed within the complex, are administered by the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee, which also oversees the langar's finances and the periodic upkeep of the gold-clad sanctum. The Amritsar GPO, from which pictorial cancellations bearing the temple's image are issued, sits only a short distance from the complex in the heart of the old city, reflecting how closely the shrine's identity is tied to the postal and civic life of Amritsar itself.
