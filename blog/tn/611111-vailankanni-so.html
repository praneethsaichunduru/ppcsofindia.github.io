Shrine Basilica of Mother Mary

அன்னை மேரி ஆலயம் (பசிலிக்கா)

Devotion at Vailankanni is traced to a cluster of reported events beginning around the mid-sixteenth century, when a local shepherd boy delivering milk is said to have encountered a woman holding a child who asked him for milk for the infant, an episode later recognized as the first apparition of Mary at the site. A second account from the close of the sixteenth century describes a lame boy who sold buttermilk beneath a banyan tree at a spot called Nadu Thittu, who was reportedly healed after the same vision appeared and asked him for curds. These oral traditions, though never formally authenticated by the Holy See, gave the settlement its enduring association with Mary under the title 'Our Lady of Good Health' and set the stage for the shrine that followed.

A third formative episode, dated to the seventeenth century, involved Portuguese sailors whose vessel, sailing from Macao toward Sri Lanka, was caught in a violent storm in the Bay of Bengal; having invoked Mary as Star of the Sea, the crew of roughly 150 men survived, and the ship made landfall on September 8, the feast of the Nativity of Mary. In gratitude, the sailors funded the rebuilding of a modest shrine near the site and continued to support it on later voyages. The shrine's Catholic significance grew steadily from this point, drawing both Christian and Hindu pilgrims in a pattern of shared devotion characteristic of Tamil Nadu, and by the twentieth century it had become one of the most visited Marian pilgrimage destinations in Asia, popularly termed the 'Lourdes of the East,' a title Pope John Paul II affirmed in 2002.

The original thatched chapel was gradually replaced by more permanent construction, and the church attained parish status in 1771 during a period when Catholic communities in the region faced pressure under Dutch colonial administration. Its present form, featuring high Gothic-style spires and vaulting distinctive among churches in Tamil Nadu, took shape chiefly through a major rebuilding campaign carried out between 1920 and 1933. Pope John XXIII formally elevated the church to the rank of minor basilica on November 3, 1962, a status that links it canonically to the major basilicas of Rome; the complex today encompasses three chapels along with ancillary buildings supporting the large volume of pilgrim traffic it receives throughout the year.

Among the shrine's distinguishing features is Matha Kullam, or Our Lady's Tank, a sacred water tank associated with the apparition accounts, where pilgrims bathe or draw water as part of their devotions. The basilica's annual festival, held over ten days from August 29 to September 8, draws crowds estimated at several million and culminates in a grand car procession on September 7 in which a flower-bedecked chariot bearing Mary's statue is drawn through the streets, traditionally with women pulling the leading car, followed by the concluding Nativity of Mary Mass on September 8. Pilgrims commonly fulfil vows during the festival period, including walking long distances on foot to the shrine and carrying offerings that blend Catholic ritual with local Tamil devotional customs such as tonsuring and the presentation of silver replicas of healed body parts.

The shrine complex today also includes a church museum displaying votive offerings left by pilgrims over the decades, a shrine basilica offering centre, Stations of the Cross and of the Rosary laid out across the grounds, and a nearby beach that forms part of the pilgrim circuit, all administered under the Roman Catholic Diocese of Thanjavur. Infrastructure around the town, including lodging and transport links tied closely to the adjoining Vailankanni Sub Post Office, has expanded considerably to accommodate the millions of annual visitors, particularly during the September festival season. The basilica functions today much as a parish and pilgrimage centre combined, with its clergy conducting regular Masses for local Catholics alongside the continuous stream of pilgrims, both Christian and Hindu, who arrive seeking the Lady of Good Health's intercession throughout the year.
