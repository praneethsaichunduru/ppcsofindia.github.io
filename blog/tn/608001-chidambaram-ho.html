Lord Nataraja

The temple's name derives from "Chit-ambalam" or "Chitrambalam," the hall of consciousness, reflecting a local legend in which Shiva, after defeating the fierce goddess Kali in a dance contest, performed his cosmic tandava here before the sages Patanjali, author of the Yoga Sutras, and Vyaghrapada, the sage with tiger feet who is said to have worshipped the original Shiva linga in a forest on this site. This mythic layer predates the surviving stone structure, and the site's association with these two sages is still commemorated in temple ritual and inscription, giving Chidambaram a founding narrative distinct from temples built primarily around royal patronage alone.

As the presiding shrine of Nataraja, Chidambaram occupies a singular place among Shaiva holy sites: it is counted among the Pancha Bhoota Sthalams representing the five elements, standing here for akasha or ether, and it is the only major Shiva temple where the principal deity is worshipped in fully anthropomorphic form rather than as an aniconic linga. Temple service has for centuries been conducted by the Dikshitars, a hereditary community of Shaiva Brahmin priests unique to Chidambaram who both perform the rituals and historically held a role in temple administration, a distinction that sets the shrine's priestly structure apart from most other South Indian temples.

Chola-period expansion transformed an earlier shrine into the present complex: Parantaka I is credited in temple tradition with gilding the roof of the Chit Sabha, the inner sanctum, using gold reportedly from a military victory, while Kulottunga I and his son Vikrama Chola undertook the major enclosure walls later known as the Kulottunga Cholan Tirumaligai, with further work carried out by their general Naralokavira. The temple as it stands comprises five sabhas or halls, five concentric prakaras (enclosures), four towering gopurams, and the Shivaganga tank, with the eastern gopuram, built under Kulottunga II in the twelfth century, carved with sculpted panels of the 108 karanas of classical dance.

The temple's most distinctive feature is the Chidambara Rahasyam, an empty space within the Chit Sabha concealed behind a curtain and marked only by strings of golden bilva leaves; when the curtain is drawn aside during worship, accompanied by conch and bell and the chanting of Tevaram hymns, devotees view not an image but the unoccupied akasha lingam, representing Shiva's formless, all-pervading aspect. This ritual moment, unique among major Hindu temples, embodies the philosophical claim that the highest form of Shiva here is no form at all, a theological statement reinforced architecturally by the golden-tiled roof of the sanctum that houses it.

The temple today anchors the annual Natyanjali dance festival, held around Maha Shivaratri in February or March, in which classical dancers from across India and abroad perform Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Odissi, and other styles as an offering to Nataraja; organized since 1981 by the Chidambaram Natyanjali Trust with support from the Ministry of Culture and the Sangeet Natak Akademi, it has become one of the subcontinent's prominent sacred dance gatherings. Management of daily worship remains with the Podu Dikshitar community even as the structure undergoes periodic conservation of its gopurams and halls, and the Chidambaram Nataraja Temple post office, serving PIN 608001 in Cuddalore district, continues to operate in the town that has grown around this shrine for over a thousand years.
